Nicolas Brosse

AI research & development — from mathematical foundations to working systems

I work in AI R&D, between research and production. After a PhD in statistics on Langevin sampling, I spent six years building deep learning systems in industry — signal processing at Thales, drug discovery at Iktos, foundation models for biology at MBZUAI and GenBio AI — and LLM systems as a freelancer. I am now a postdoctoral researcher at CREST with Arnak Dalalyan, working on two things: the mathematics of generative models, and human–AI collaboration in mathematical research, through Human–AI Mathematics, an open framework in which AI agents search for and cross-review proofs that mathematicians can check. What I care about is taking an idea from paper to working system, and measuring whether it actually works.

Human–AI Mathematics is an open framework with two aims: a structure that keeps an agent-driven search traceable, where a claim moves to proved only through an independent review of its exact text, and a manuscript that mathematicians can read, check and contribute to. Its first case study is the KLS conjecture, which three teams proved while the search was running.

Zooming in at scale σ replaces the support by its inward tangent cone: its Gaussian mass gives the leading density, and derivatives of its logarithm give the leading singular fields — a manifold-valued inverse Mills ratio.
